How to read this

Each bar or dot is one of 19 large Indian cities; the data counts people arrested in 2020 by age group and sex.

Counts vs. shares. A count is the number of arrests. A share is a group’s part of that city’s own arrests. Counts show where the volume is; shares show where a group is over-represented, so a small city can rank low on counts but high on shares.

Using the views. Open the controls (☰) to choose an age bracket, gender and cities. The pyramid shows counts (men left, women right). In the scatter plot, read height against the dashed 19-city average; cities far above or below it are worth a closer look, and diamond points mark a count of zero. The table lists zero or very low counts.

What this doesn’t show

  • Arrests are not crimes. Figures reflect policing and recording practice as well as crime. More arrests does not mean a more dangerous city.
  • No offence type, charge, bail or conviction data. Serious and minor cases cannot be separated.
  • No population adjustment. The table has no population figures, so cities of different sizes are not directly comparable.
  • Who is missing. Anyone not arrested, unreported crime, and all places outside these 19 cities.
  • Possible reporting gaps. Zero or near-zero cells (e.g. Ghaziabad, Kanpur and Patna record no arrests of people aged 60+) may reflect how data was recorded, not an absence of arrests. Treat flags as questions, not findings.
  • Single year. 2020 was a COVID-19 year, so patterns may not hold in other years.
